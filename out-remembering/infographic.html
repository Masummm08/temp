3Science

The research backs it up

Kids with better working memory do better at maths, even at the same IQ.

2010Alloway & Alloway

Memory at 5 predicted maths at 11, better than IQ.

2011Alloway & Passolunghi

Memory adds something verbal skill doesn’t.

2013Friso-van den Bos et al.

Big review: the link shows up again and again.

2015Blankenship et al.

Still matters after controlling for IQ and age.

The fine print

Memory and IQ overlap a lot, and stats can’t fully pull them apart. And no, brain-training apps won’t make you a maths genius.

4Notebook

AI thinks out loud, in a giant notebook

Its writing isn’t a report of the thinking. The writing is the thinking.

Humans win at
  • Holding a number silently in your head
  • Updating it without writing anything
AI wins at
  • Keeping hundreds of notes word for word
  • Looking back at any of them, any time
Is it really “memory”?

Not quite. AI can’t edit what it already wrote and sometimes misses details in long notes. The author calls it augmented symbolic working memory: weaker inside, far bigger outside.

5Why maths

Maths is perfect for this

Everything in maths can be written down, and it stays put.

Most maths mistakes aren’t dumb. They’re bookkeeping.

6Long proofs

Long proofs break human brains

You can follow every step of a 100-step proof and still lose the thread.

“Thinking longer” may really mean searching more inside a bigger notebook.

7Real life

Real life is messier

More memory can’t find facts nobody ever saw.

Maths

  • All the facts are on the page
  • Words mean exactly one thing
  • You can check the answer

Life

  • Key facts are hidden
  • “Fair” means different things
  • You may never know if you were right

“Why did Maria stop texting?” Angry? Busy? Lost her phone? No amount of memory tells you.

10Big picture

Von Neumann, not Einstein

Today’s AI is fast and broad. It isn’t yet deep and original.

AI today

von Neumann

  • Super fast
  • Remembers everything
  • Follows any argument
vs.
Still missing

Einstein

  • Throws out the old frame
  • Sees the hidden mistake
  • Invents a new view

Physicist Eugene Wigner knew both: von Neumann was the quickest mind he met, Einstein the deepest.

What to watch for next

AI that notices a problem was framed wrong, and invents a better way to see it.
