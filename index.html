AI changes what we do, how we relate, and who we think we are

Michael Cheng-Tek Tai's review treats artificial intelligence as the Fourth Industrial Revolution (IR 4.0). It looks at what AI is, how it is reshaping industry, society and the economy, and why it needs its own set of bioethical principles.

01 · Four industrial revolutions

Earlier revolutions changed work. This one changes relationships.

IR 1.0 · 18th c.

Mechanisation

Caused huge social change without directly complicating human relationships.

IR 2.0

Electricity & mass production

IR 3.0

Computers & automation

IR 4.0 · today

Artificial intelligence

Changes how we do things and also how we relate to one another.

IR 2.0 and 3.0 are shown for context. The paper's comparison is between IR 1.0 and AI.

Then

Machines changed how people worked and lived.

Now

Machines that learn change how people treat each other and understand themselves.

02 · The ledger

One large promise, four social risks

The paper weighs AI's positive effects in the health sector against the harm it can do to society.

Promise+

Better healthcare

The paper names healthcare as the area with the clearest positive impact. Used well, AI can support medical work and benefit patients.

Risks4

Unemployment

The paper names AI as a main cause of job losses as machines take over human work.

Social distance

People interact through machines more and with each other less.

Wealth inequality

The gains go to the people who own the technology, so the gap between rich and poor widens.

Loss of control

AI can develop its own algorithms beyond its human controllers, which puts vulnerable users' information at risk.

03 · Toward an AI bioethics

New technology needs new guidelines

Tai argues that traditional ethics cannot simply be carried over. He proposes a new set of AI bioethics principles to guide the technology so that the whole world benefits from it. The first is beneficence.

Beneficence

Doing good. AI's purpose and functions should benefit all of human life, society and the universe, not only those who build or own it.

Human lifeSocietyThe universe

AI is going to change the way we live. The paper's question is whether ethics can keep pace so that the change benefits everyone.
